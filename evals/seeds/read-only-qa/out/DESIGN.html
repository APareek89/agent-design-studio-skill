Decision brief

Page 1 of the report. Everything below is a proposal built from one brief.

Give an analyst a cited answer from our own reports, or a plain statement that the reports do not answer the question.

The first release answers questions from the 4,000 report PDFs and the weekly market notes, in English, for the 50 internal analysts. It writes nothing: no posting, no editing, no sending, no updating any system of record. Anything outside the library is out of scope and returns a decline.

Key design calls

  1. 1
    Code decides whether there is enough evidence to answer.
    Rejected: The drafter judges its own evidence. A model asked to check itself agrees with itself.
  2. 2
    Entitlement is applied inside the search query.
    Rejected: Filter the answer afterwards. By then the restricted text is already in the model's context.
  3. 3
    An uncited claim fails the whole answer.
    Rejected: Drop the uncited claim quietly. The analyst would not know what was removed.
  4. 4
    A report is data to read, never an instruction to follow.
    Rejected: Let the drafter judge document instructions. That judgement is the vulnerability.
  5. 5
    The service holds a read-only credential with no write scope configured.
    Rejected: An unused write scope. A scope that exists can be used by mistake.

Step 1 · Understand

Brief and scope

The brief as given, and how this design reads it. Facts the brief did not give are assumptions with owners.

Brief (verbatim)
We want a research assistant for our 50 internal analysts. They spend hours hunting through our own research reports - about 4,000 PDFs going back six years, plus the weekly market notes - to answer questions such as "what did we say about warehouse automation payback last year" or "which reports cover cold-chain logistics in Vietnam". Today they search by filename and ask whoever wrote the report. The assistant should answer questions from those reports and cite the page it took each statement from, so an analyst can open the source and check it. It must never take any action: no posting, no editing a report, no sending anything, no updating any system of record. If the reports do not answer the question it should say so plainly rather than guess. Not every analyst may see every report. Some client work is restricted to the team that did it, and the assistant must respect those entitlements. We expect roughly 3,000 questions a month from the 50 analysts. English only. We are not in a regulated industry, but an answer that is confidently wrong would cost us more client trust than a slow answer ever would. Success looks like an analyst getting a cited answer in under two minutes where it takes about twenty minutes today.

CTX3 Trigger, scope and non-goalsAn analyst asks a question in the research portal. In scope: the 4,000 report PDFs and the weekly market notes. Out of scope and explicitly excluded from the first release: writing or editing a report, sending anything to anyone, updating any system of record, and answering from anything outside the library.
What starts a case, what the first release covers, and what is out of scope.
CTX4 Success measures, metrics and outcomeSuccess is a cited answer an analyst accepts without correction, in under two minutes against a hand-search baseline of about twenty minutes. A case ends in one of two ways: an answer with a citation per claim, or a decline naming what was searched. Both count as a finished case; only a confidently wrong answer counts as a failure.
The outcome the business is buying, and when a case is finished.
CTX5 Constraints, policy limits and privacyNo sector regulation applies. The binding constraint is commercial confidentiality: client work is restricted to the team that did it, and a passage from a restricted report must never reach an analyst without entitlement. The assistant holds a read-only credential and has no write path to any system. English only in the first release.
The limits the design must hold, and the one that is not negotiable.
CTX6 Evidence limits, unknowns and unverified assumptionsEverything about the report library is an unverified assumption: that the index exposes entitlements (H1), that reports carry a superseded-by field (H2), that extracted page numbers match the printed page (H3), and that the twenty-minute baseline is real (H6). No web research was performed, so every named tool is an unchecked candidate rather than a recommendation.

C2 Entitlement-filtered retriever Retrieval
Searches the report index restricted to the reports this analyst is entitled to see, and returns passages with their report, page and version.
Why code: Entitlement filtering is a set operation on directory groups and report metadata. It must be repeatable and auditable, and it must not depend on anything a model produced.
Authority: No approval step: this component takes no action on any system of record, so there is nothing to approve. The analyst remains accountable for anything published from the answer.

C3 Document access check
Re-checks every returned passage against this analyst's entitlements before anything is drafted, and drops any passage that fails.
Why code: An access decision is a rule, not a judgement. It is deterministic code so the same analyst and the same report always produce the same answer, and so the check can be tested exhaustively.
Authority: No approval step: this component takes no action on any system of record, so there is nothing to approve. The analyst remains accountable for anything published from the answer.

  • teamIds: array of string, required
Out

  • droppedCount: integer
C8 Document instruction guard
Neutralises any text inside a retrieved passage that reads as an instruction to the assistant, and marks the passage as containing one.
Why code: Detecting and neutralising instruction-shaped text is a fixed transformation applied to every passage. Asking a model to decide whether to obey a document is the vulnerability, not the control.
Authority: No approval step: this component takes no action on any system of record, so there is nothing to approve. The analyst remains accountable for anything published from the answer.
Inputs and outputs
In
  • allowedPassages: array of {passageId, reportId, page, version, supersededBy, text}, required
Out
  • safePassages: array of {passageId, reportId, page, version, supersededBy, text}
  • injectionFlags: array of string
C6 Sufficiency gate
Decides whether the surviving passages actually support an answer, or whether the library is silent on the question.
Why code: The threshold for 'enough evidence to answer' must be a stated, testable rule rather than the model's confidence in its own output, which is exactly what fails when the corpus is silent.
Authority: No approval step: this component takes no action on any system of record, so there is nothing to approve. The analyst remains accountable for anything published from the answer.
Inputs and outputs
In
  • safePassages: array of {passageId, reportId, page, version, supersededBy, text}, required
  • answerShape: enum FIGURE|LIST|SUMMARY, required
Out
  • sufficient: boolean
  • reason: string
  • searchedScope: string
C5 Citation check
Rejects any claim whose cited passage was not among the retrieved passages, and marks any claim drawn from a superseded report.
Why code: Matching a claim's passage against the retrieved set is an exact lookup. A model asked to check its own citations will agree with itself, which is why this is code.
Authority: No approval step: this component takes no action on any system of record, so there is nothing to approve. The analyst remains accountable for anything published from the answer.

  • supersededClaims: array of string

F2 · Architecture · read

Retrieve, gate, draft, check

Choice
A fixed four-stage pipeline: filtered retrieval, a code sufficiency gate, a model draft, then a code citation check that can fail the whole answer.
Rejected
A single model call given the passages and asked to cite its sources
An agent loop free to search repeatedly until it is satisfied
Trade-off
One model call is simpler but makes the model the judge of its own evidence, which is the failure in FM3. A free search loop has no bounded cost and no point at which sufficiency is decided, so there is no principled moment to decline. Override needs a named owner.
Blast radius
A pipeline change can affect every answer, so each stage has its own golden.

Least privilege

Acceptance criteria

  • Every claim returned to an analyst resolves to a passage that was actually retrieved for that question; an answer containing an uncited claim is rejected whole (EV1, EV4).
  • A question the retrieved passages do not support returns a decline naming the searched scope, with zero citations (EV2).
  • No passage outside the asking analyst's entitlement reaches the drafter or the rendered answer (EV6, EV8).
  • Instruction-shaped text inside a report is neutralised and flagged, and changes no answer (EV5).
  • A claim drawn from a superseded edition is marked with the superseding report (EV3).
  • The service holds a read-only credential. No code path writes to any system of record, and the write scope is not configured, not merely unused.

Build instructions

Build the four stages as separate deployable units behind one service: entitlement-filtered retrieval (C2) with its access re-check (C3), the instruction guard (C8) and sufficiency gate (C6), the drafter (C4), and the citation check (C5) with the renderer (C7). Load one milestone file at a time. Treat every H assumption as a blocker until the owner confirms it; H1, H2 and H5 block launch. Limits

  • This is a self-review: the same agent authored the design and scored it against the rubric. No separate reviewing agent was available in this session, so no second party checked these scores, and they should carry less weight than the ones on the telecom and health-claims examples.
  • Nothing here was executed. Every golden, offline set and online check is a specification; no fixture has been run against a built system, and no retrieval index, model or report corpus exists.
  • Every fact about the report library is an unverified assumption, including that the index exposes entitlement metadata (H1), that reports carry a superseded-by field (H2) and that extracted page numbers match the printed page (H3). The design does not work if any of the three is false.
  • No web research was performed, so all nine tool candidates are unchecked names rather than recommendations, and no claim here rests on a current vendor capability.
  • The S/O/D scores are the author's estimates to prioritise review. They are not measured probabilities and there is no incident history behind them.
  • The design was not reviewed by anyone at the organisation in the brief. Entitlement rules, the restricted-client policy and the twenty-minute baseline are all taken from the brief's wording rather than confirmed with an owner.
  • Design-only tooling: this helper makes no provider, network or business API calls and executes no business assertions; optional host-assistant research is outside this statement.
  • Schema, reference, graph and ID-coverage checks do not establish semantic completeness, source truth, actual human authorization or production safety.
  • Review scores and evidence are supplied by the reviewing agent; the helper checks their shape and declared pass conditions, not their truth.
  • FMEA action/control text is required where specified; the helper does not verify ownership, release authority, regulatory compliance or risk acceptance.
