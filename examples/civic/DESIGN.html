Streetlight repair intake and operator-assisted routing

Page 1 of the report. Everything below is a proposal built from one brief.

Help city staff route streetlight reports and track each saved ticket.

Staff can create or link a repair ticket. The system cannot switch lights or control street equipment.

Key design calls

  1. 1
    Staff approve ticket changes.
    Rejected: Free agent writes. People own routing
  2. 2
    Reuse known tickets.
    Rejected: New ticket each time. Avoid double work
  3. 3
    No equipment control.
    Rejected: Remote switching. Keep the first release safe

Step 1 · Understand

Brief and scope

The brief as given, and how this design reads it. Facts the brief did not give are assumptions with owners.

Brief (verbatim)
I need a system for a small city team to handle streetlight repair requests: classify reports, find existing tickets and route to the right maintenance crew. Five operators handle about 200 reports a day. It must not remotely control equipment.
FieldDesign reading
Scope First releaseStaff can create or link a repair ticket. The system cannot switch lights or control street equipment.
CTX1 Decision and first releaseRecommend the category, existing-ticket candidates and responsible crew for each operator-entered report. An operator confirms CREATE or LINK before a ticket-ledger write. Completion is a durable ticket/link receipt or a visible owned exception; a recommendation alone is never dispatched work. Reject autonomous dispatch/remote diagnosis as unnecessary authority at this scale.
CTX2 Actors, trigger and capacityFive operators handle about 200 reports a day, from the user brief. Trigger is an authenticated operator submitting a text report. Proposed roles: duty supervisor owns urgent and ambiguous cases; maintenance lead owns route policy; IT lead owns adapters and recovery. 40 reports/operator/day is only an equal-allocation arithmetic average; shift length and peak arrival rate are unknown. Baseline captures these before capacity claims.
CTX3 Inputs, outcomes and exclusionsInputs: city/session claims, intake ID/revision/text, optional asset ID/address, authoritative asset/ticket/crew snapshots. Outputs: category, evidence, candidates, proposed crew, operator decision, durable ticket/link or owned queue case. No equipment connectivity, switching, reset, dimming, telemetry commands, work completion claims, citizen messaging, autonomous ticket closure, procurement or crew scheduling optimization. Cancellation cancels a pending proposal only; existing committed tickets are handed to a human for separately authorized handling.
CTX4 Human dispositions and service windowsUrgent reports are surfaced immediately even if information is missing. C8 durably owns every exception with supervisor, reason, status and next due time; C5 records ACK/APPROVE/REJECT/CANCEL/OVERDUE. Urgent acknowledgement uses elapsed clock, routine timers use the published staffed calendar. No confirmed out-of-hours procedure means no real launch; no promise that software substitutes for emergency response.
CTX5 Value and measurementR1 defines handling minutes/report and correct first-route fraction; lower search/edit time is the intended benefit. R2 guards urgent detection and safe ticket writes. Unknown baseline must be collected. No repair-time or public-safety improvement claim follows from a triage recommendation; repair outcomes can be a secondary descriptive signal.
CTX6 Authority and integration boundaryProposed adapters remain launch dependencies. No actual city policy, API or provider has been verified. Before adapter authority is proven, implementation may persist internal proposed cases and read approved synthetic/sandbox data only. Remote equipment APIs are absent from schemas, credentials, network allowlist and model tools.
